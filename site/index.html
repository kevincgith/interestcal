Cash flows

Each amount on its date.Each amount at its period: 0 = now (T0), 1 = T+1, and so on. Use a minus sign for money paid out (e.g. −100,000) to get a net present value. An amount dated before the valuation date is grown forward to it at the same rate. A repeating cash flow (e.g. rent each month) starts on its date and keeps that day of the month. A repeating cash flow comes once a period from its first period.

      Interest: simple (or compounded) interest at the judgment debt rate published by the HK Judiciary, the HSBC best lending (prime) rate published by the HKMA and cross-checked daily against HSBC’s official prime rate page, the US prime rate from the Federal Reserve’s H.15 release, or a fixed rate, with optional principal added later and partial payments. By default both the start and end dates earn interest (End date included), the usual court convention, so 1 to 2 January is 2 days; each day earns interest once, and where one period ends and the next begins the shared date is counted once. Choose “End date not included” under Days counted to count from the start date up to, but not including, the end date (end date minus start date, as in Excel and the original workbook). Interest for each period = principal × rate × days ÷ year days, where the year days depend on the day count basis you choose.

      Mortgage: equal monthly instalments (interest = balance × rate × days ÷ 365) at a prime-based, HIBOR-based (1-month or 3-month HIBOR, set by HKAB, history from the HKMA) or fixed rate.

      PV: the present value of cash flows by dates or periods (T0, T+1, …), or the rate that makes it zero (IRR), as in Excel’s XNPV, XIRR, NPV and IRR; and a time value of money calculator (N, I/Y, PV, PMT, FV) as on a financial calculator.

      Inflation: what an amount at one time is worth at another, by the Composite Consumer Price Index published by the Census and Statistics Department, with its history since 1980.
